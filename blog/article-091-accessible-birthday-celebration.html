How to Make a Birthday Celebration More Accessible

Plan a birthday that is easier to navigate and enjoy by checking routes, seating, restrooms, sound, lighting, and event information with guests.

Short answer: Ask guests privately what would help, then verify the route and setup from arrival through departure. Accessibility is individual, so use this checklist as a starting point rather than an assumption.

Map the guest journey

Think about how guests reach the door, enter the room, move between seats and food, use the restroom, and leave. Check steps, thresholds, heavy doors, narrow routes, lighting, distances between stations, and a place to pause. At a hired venue, ask staff specific questions and request current access information.

Before guests arrive, walk the route yourself. Keep bags, cables, gifts, and decorations out of the path. Place stable seating near the group and make water and food reachable.

Offer choices without singling people out

Guests may prefer different sound levels, seating, or break options. Share event details in advance and make a quieter area available to anyone. Do not ask someone to explain a disability in front of the group.

A private note can say: “I want the setup to work for you. Is there anything about access, seating, sound, or timing that would help?”

Make access checks part of setup

Ask a helper to check the entrance, route, signs, lighting, and music volume before the party. Keep exits clear and tell venue staff which paths need to remain open. If a feature is uncertain, say so and share a contact who can confirm it.

Accessibility checklist

  • Ask privately what would make attendance easier.
  • Confirm entrance, route, restroom, seating, and venue details.
  • Keep paths and exits clear throughout the event.
  • Share transport, parking, and access information early.
  • Identify a point person and a quiet break place.

Frequently asked questions

Should I ask guests about access needs?

Yes. Ask privately and make the question optional. Focus on what would help rather than requesting medical details.

What if I cannot verify an access feature?

Say what you know and what is uncertain. Offer a venue contact or another practical option so the guest can decide.
