Birthday Decoration Ideas: Number Balloons in Photos

Planning birthday decorations or photos? Number balloons can make a celebration feel personal. Here are easy ways to place and photograph them, with practical tips for light, setup and safety.

Quick answer

Stand gold number balloons beside you at about hip or seat height, not in front of your face, and pick a plain background so the gold stands out. Light from the side, not from straight ahead, to cut glare. Keep foil balloons away from candles, heaters and sockets.

What colour balloons work with your outfit?

Choose a balloon colour that contrasts a little with what you are wearing. Gold works with lilac, blush, cream, black, green and deep red. It can blend into yellow or mustard, so avoid that pairing if you want the balloons to stand out. Silver suits blue and cool-toned outfits. If you have a cake, check that its colour fits the same palette, because the cake and balloons will often sit in the same frame. The complementary colours idea is a simple guide: warm gold beside a cool lilac makes both look clearer. For more help choosing, see how to choose a birthday color palette.

Where should number balloons go in the frame?

Place them beside you, not in front of your face. Standing them on the floor or on a low surface at hip or seat height keeps the focus on you and lets the number read clearly. Leave a small gap between the digits so each shape stays visible, and check that the number reads left to right, with no digit hiding behind another. A simple composition is to put yourself in one third of the frame and the balloons in another, following the rule of thirds. Take one wide shot to show the full setup and one closer shot where the balloons are partly cropped and the face is clear.

How do you stop gold balloons looking glary?

Foil is a mirror, so it reflects windows, lamps and your phone flash. Switch off the flash. Put the main light, such as a window, to the side of the balloons, not behind the camera, which makes a bright white patch on the front. Shift two or three steps left or right until the glare slides off. Wipe fingerprints with a dry cloth, because smudges show up on shiny surfaces. If the gold turns pale in the picture, tap on your face and lower the brightness slightly. The idea behind this is specular reflection: smooth, shiny surfaces bounce light straight back, so the angle matters more than the amount of light.

Are foil balloons safe to use indoors?

Mostly, with some rules. Foil balloons are a thin plastic film with a metal coating, and that coating conducts electricity. Keep them away from sockets, extension leads and power lines. Keep them away from candles, flames and heaters, which can pop them or melt the film. Never release a foil balloon outdoors, since it can drift into power lines and cause an outage. Put a weight on the string or tie it to something heavy, and let the air out before you throw a balloon away. For a general overview, see the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, and check the instructions printed on the pack. The guide to keeping birthday decorations safe and stable covers more.

How do you keep balloons from drifting?

If you fill them with helium, tie each string to a small weight, a heavy mug or the leg of a chair. Number balloons are often sold ready to fill with air or helium, so check the pack. Air-filled ones do not float, so they stay where you put them, and they can lean against a sofa, a wall or a box. Keep balloons away from fans, air-conditioning vents and open doors, which push them out of position between shots. Inflate on the day, ideally an hour or two before the photos, so they look full and smooth. For a small room, the guide to a balloon focal point without filling the room shows how few balloons you need.

What simple backdrop works with gold balloons?

Gold looks best against something calm: a plain wall, a sofa in one colour, or a curtain. Move cables, bottles and bright clutter out of the frame. A soft rug or a plain cloth on the floor under the balloons gives the number a clean base and hides any floor pattern. Choose matte surfaces nearby, since a shiny table next to foil adds even more reflections. If you only have a patterned wall, move the shoot to a corner with fewer details, or crop in closer.

Frequently asked questions

What size number balloons are best for photos?

Larger ones read better from a distance, but they need room. Check the size on the pack, then stand them next to you and take a test photo. If the numbers cover your face or crowd the edge of the frame, move them lower or use a smaller size.

Can I use air instead of helium?

Often yes. Many foil number balloons can be filled with air, using a straw, and they stay where you put them. They do not float, so lean them against a wall or sofa. Check the instructions on the pack.

Is it fine to use foil balloons outdoors?

Use them with care. Wind can pull them away, and a loose foil balloon can drift into power lines. Weight or tie them securely, and never release them. Take them inside when the shoot ends.
