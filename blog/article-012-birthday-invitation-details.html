What to Put on a Birthday Invitation

A helpful birthday invitation answers the practical questions before guests need to ask: when, where, how to reply, what to expect, and how to ask about access needs.

In brief

Include the date, start and finish time, address or meeting point, RSVP method and deadline, dress guidance if useful, and arrival or accessibility details.

Put the essentials first

Make the date, start time, location, host contact, and RSVP details easy to find. If there is a planned finish time, state it. Give one reply route and a clear deadline.

For a venue, include its name and a map link or entrance note. For a home gathering, share the address privately when needed and add parking or door directions.

Add details that help guests prepare

Mention the event style, whether food is served, and practical expectations. If plans include dancing, a garden, stairs, or a walk, say so plainly.

Offer a private way to ask about access or dietary needs. Share only the information guests need; do not put sensitive details in a public post.

Make the invite easy to scan

Use short labels and line breaks. If details change, send a direct update to all guests rather than relying on a social post they may not see.

Quick checklist

  • Event name and host.
  • Date, start/end time, and location.
  • Map, arrival instructions, and access notes.
  • RSVP method and reply-by date.
  • Food, dress vibe, and what guests should bring, if anything.

Frequently asked questions

Should I include an end time?

It helps guests plan transport and is especially useful for venue bookings or shared homes.

How should I mention accessibility?

State known details such as steps, a step-free route, seating, or restroom access and invite private questions.
