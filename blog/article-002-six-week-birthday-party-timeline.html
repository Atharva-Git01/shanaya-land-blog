A Six-Week Birthday Party Planning Timeline

A six-week runway makes the decisions easier by handling bookings first and leaving the final week for practical preparation.

Quick answer: Confirm the date, guest count, and setting first; invite guests early; arrange food and cake next; then use the final week to gather supplies and prepare the room.

Six weeks out: settle the big decisions

Choose the celebration style, date, time, approximate guest count, and a spending limit. Decide whether to host at home or check venue availability. If booking a venue, ask about capacity, setup access, included furniture, decoration rules, cancellation terms, and extra charges before paying.

Five weeks out: confirm the setting and invite

Book the venue or commit to the home layout. Send invitations with the date, start and end time, address, RSVP method, and useful access or arrival notes. Set a reply-by date that leaves time to estimate food and seating.

Four weeks out: plan food, cake, and the look

Choose whether guests are coming for a meal, snacks, or dessert. If ordering a custom cake, share the serving count, flavor, colors, reference photo, dietary needs, pickup time, and transport plan. Select a small palette that can connect the outfit and decor.

Three weeks out: check the details

Confirm music, seating, serving dishes, lighting, and any activity. Ask a helper to greet guests or capture photos. Recheck venue rules and weather plans for an outdoor event. Borrow or buy only the supplies that are still missing.

Two weeks to party week

Review replies and send one friendly follow-up to guests who have not answered. Adjust food and seating plans to the likely count. Write a flexible run sheet for setup, arrivals, food, photos, cake, and cleanup.

During the final week, buy shelf-stable items, charge devices, collect decor and serving tools in one place, and confirm cake pickup. The day before, tidy the room, set out nonperishable supplies, chill what needs chilling, and send a short arrival reminder.

Frequently asked questions

Is six weeks necessary for a small party?

No. Treat this as a sequence, not a rule. A small gathering can follow the same order on a shorter timeline.

When should invitations go out?

Send them once the date, time, and setting are reliable. Include a reply-by date so you can plan seating and food.
